Before we begin

A few practical questions.

What does a project cost?

I quote a fixed project fee after reviewing the scope. The proposal identifies what’s included, any third-party costs, and what would require a separate agreement. You can decide whether it fits before committing.

Do we need an existing audience?

Usually, yes. This service is designed around an audience you can reach, such as members, customers, visitors, or program participants. If you need outside recruitment, we discuss feasibility and costs before proceeding.

What if we already have survey results?

That can be a separate analysis project. I’ll first review the questionnaire, data format, and research question to determine whether the existing responses can support a useful report.

Will I need new software or ongoing support?

Not necessarily. I start with the tools and data you already have. Any new subscriptions, integrations, access requirements, or ongoing maintenance responsibilities are discussed in the proposal. Tool subscriptions and continuing support are not automatically included.

Can you guarantee a particular outcome?

No. Research can reduce uncertainty, and better workflows and reports can support your team, but none guarantees a business outcome. Research limitations, data quality issues, platform constraints, and maintenance responsibilities are made clear within the project.

How do communication and revisions work?

We agree on one primary contact, scheduled updates, review dates, and the number of revision rounds in the proposal. Additional requests are scoped separately. Each engagement ends with an agreed handoff; ongoing support is a separate decision.
