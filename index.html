Before we begin

A few practical questions.

How do I know which service I need?

Start with what isn’t working. Research helps you understand your audience; workflow projects improve how work moves through your team; reporting projects make information easier to use. If the problem crosses more than one area, we’ll identify a focused starting point together.

Can you help with a new survey or results we already have?

Both. I can design a new study around a specific question or analyze existing survey responses. For a new study, we discuss who you can reach and how to collect responses. For existing results, I review the questionnaire and data first to see what they can support.

Can you improve a workflow without replacing all our tools?

Yes. I start by understanding your current steps, tools, owners, and handoffs. The project may involve simplifying the process, cleaning up Monday.com or Asana, or adding supported automations. Any new software, subscriptions, or integrations are discussed before we agree on the work.

Can you rebuild a report using our existing data?

Yes. I review your current reports, source data, and the decisions the report needs to support. We agree on the metrics and format, then I rebuild the dashboard, spreadsheet, or presentation and document how to refresh it. Any gaps in the data are identified before we settle the scope.

Do you just use AI?

My work starts with understanding your problem, your data, and how your team operates. AI can be a useful tool when applied thoughtfully. If it’s the right fit, I’ll recommend it and help you implement it. My role is to choose a practical approach, check the quality of the work, and turn it into something your team can use.

What does a project cost?

I quote a fixed project fee after reviewing the scope, whether it’s research, workflow improvement, or reporting. The proposal explains the deliverables, review rounds, any third-party costs, and what would require additional work. You can decide whether it fits before committing.

How does a project start, and how long will it take?

Send a short description of the problem, your current tools or data, and any deadline. We agree on the outcome, scope, timeline, and review points before work begins. Timing depends on the project’s complexity and the availability of the information, access, and feedback needed.

What happens at the end of the project?

You receive the agreed deliverables, supporting documentation, and a walkthrough. That might be a research report and recommendations, an improved workflow, or a rebuilt report with refresh instructions. We agree on revision rounds and responsibilities up front; ongoing support is discussed separately.
